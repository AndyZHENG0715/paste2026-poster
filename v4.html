PASTE 2026 · Student Poster · Innovation Project

Two-Stage Water Rocket: From AI Simulation to Real-World Experiment

Transcultural & transectoral collaboration in transdisciplinary education
Zian Zheng · Shijie Wu · Yuxi Jia · Lucia Luo
Hong Kong Baptist University — Sustainable Service Education Group

Background

Teaching abstract mechanics to young children is hard. We designed a bilingual (Chinese–English) STEM unit for primary-school students that pairs an AI simulation platform with a real-world build-and-launch experiment — delivered as a cross-regional service-learning project by Hong Kong university students.

Transdisciplinary physics × engineering × AI × pedagogy   Transectoral university ↔ school ↔ community   Transcultural Hong Kong ↔ Jiangxi, bilingual

Objectives

Knowledge

Explain launch principles, pressure vs. air pressure, action–reaction, acceleration and air resistance.

Ability

Practise hypothesis → test → optimise; variable control; data recording; engineering design.

Transdisciplinary

Couple simulation with hands-on experiment, transferable to other STEM topics.

Approach & Methodology

1
Learn
Rocket basics · two-stage structure · pressure
2
Build
Rocket assembly · variable control
3
Simulate
AI digital-twin parameter sweep
4
Launch
Formal launch · measure & record
5
Reflect
Simulation vs. real · optimise

The AI simulation platform acts as a pre-launch “digital twin”: students sweep water volume, air pressure and nozzle diameter to predict the optimal launch before the physical test — reducing cost and safety risk while grounding intuition in theory.

Key Findings & Implications

1
Simulation deepened understanding. The digital twin made abstract forces visible — students saw how changing water volume, air pressure or nozzle diameter changed the launch in real time. This grounded action–reaction and pressure in tangible cause-and-effect, so [X]% of learners could explain the underlying mechanism before launching.
2
Simulation informed the real experiment. The platform's predicted optimal parameters directly guided the physical launches: students applied the simulated settings and brought the observed trajectory closer to the predicted one across [M] launches, reducing trial-and-error.
3
Impact & next steps. Learners gain mechanics intuition and inquiry skills; the school & community gain a reusable, low-cost STEM module. Next: map physics one-to-one onto the build/launch steps and add a student innovation zone to close the loop learn → practise → create.
